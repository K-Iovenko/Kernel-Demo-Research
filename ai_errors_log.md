# Where the AI was wrong

The analysis was done in stages: first on the three primary statements, then with the notes. Each AI claim about a cause was checked against the annual report. These are the errors and omissions that were found and corrected.

| # | What the AI said | What the report shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Wrong.** The bonds became current because they fall due within 12 months (repeated twice). | Maturity is October 2027. The reclassification follows from cross-acceleration with a loan under a waiver (IAS 1, para. 69(d)). | Note 22 |
| 2 | **Overstated risk.** "A covenant is breached, the whole debt structure depends on a temporary waiver." | A non-financial covenant (war-related material adverse event) is breached on a USD 70.3m loan. Financial covenants are met; the waiver was extended to 30 September 2027. | Note 21 |
| 3 | **Missed.** The increase in short-term borrowings is only new credit lines and debt taken over with Enselco. | Another USD 70.3m is a long-term loan reclassified as short-term because of the waiver. | Notes 21, 22 |
| 4 | **Wrong.** The gap between cash in the cash flow statement and the balance sheet is probably an expected credit loss allowance. | The gap is bank overdrafts (USD 2k and 3k). The allowance is immaterial. | Note 9 |
| 5 | **Dropped a correct guess too early.** First suggested lower customer prepayments, then called it wrong after reading Note 19. | Management confirms lower prepayments. The decrease consists of advances taken over with Enselco, commission liabilities and litigation provisions. | p. 10, Notes 8, 19 |
| 6 | **Too optimistic.** "Cash and undrawn lines (USD 853.6m) cover all current debt." | The lines are mostly short-term or earmarked. Reliable liquidity is USD 368.7m against USD 887.1m of payments due within one year. | p. 11, Note 32 |
| 7 | **Inconsistent.** Mixed amounts at the original scale and after dividing by the coefficient in one answer. | All amounts were brought to one scale. | All notes |

## Confirmed

The main driver of net debt growth is the cash acquisition (about 95% of the increase); the estimate of about USD 110m of debt taken over with Enselco was close (actual USD 97.5m); the origin of the jumps in intangible assets and lease liabilities; and comfortable interest cover. The gap between EBITDA / finance costs in the model (6.44x) and the company's metric (10.2x) is a difference in method, not an error.

## Still unconfirmed

- Enselco's profit after the acquisition is mainly a non-cash revaluation of crops.
- Enselco was loss-making before the acquisition (estimate from pro forma figures).
- The commission-agreement liability was owed to a related party (based on matching amounts).
